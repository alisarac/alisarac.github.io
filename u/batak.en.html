Batak

Turkish trick-taking: four modes, twenty-two regulars, every hand read back.

Live

A table, not a slot machine.

What it does

Read your hand back

After every hand the game shows it to you again, card by card: what you played, what the engine would have played, and why. It answers the question you faced, from the cards you could see — it does not look at anyone's hand — and it tells you how sure it is: “better in forty deals, worse in none”.

The regulars

A cast of twenty-two, each with a face, a temper and a history with you. A different three sit down each game; pin the ones you like. Who makes their contract as your partner, who you take the hand from — it is all kept.

The rules you play by

Every house has its own Batak. Minimum bid 4, 5 or 6; what happens when everyone passes; trump in the first trick; leading trump before it is broken; the “must beat the trick” rule; double penalties; the game to 51 or 101 or a fixed number of hands; no-trump bids. Each has a one-line explanation.

Opponents that play the game

Three levels. The hard one samples thousands of possible deals before every card and bids by expected points; it remembers who is out of which suit. Easy is forgiving, normal is a fair table.

Taught at the table

Your first hands explain themselves: when a card cannot be played the game says why — follow suit, beat the trick, play trump. The last trick stays visible, and the score is kept the way it is kept on paper.

Honest money

Free; an advert only between hands, never during play, and no rewarded video. One purchase removes them and opens every hand read back, the full record and four more table looks. Nothing that wins a trick is for sale — the bots play exactly as hard.
