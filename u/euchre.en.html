Euchre

Classic Euchre with partners: order it up, go alone, euchre the other side.

Live

A table up north.

What it does

A pale pine table

The score is kept the old way, with a four and a six. Each seat's slip says what it called — pass, order up, alone — and the tricks it has taken. When trump is made, the left bower jumps into your trumps so you see the rule happen. A dark mode turns the lamp off.

Four ways to play

Classic: partners across the table, the turn-up and two rounds of calling. Canadian Loner: the dealer's partner can order it up only by going alone. Benny: a joker joins the deck as the highest trump of all. Cutthroat: three players, each on their own.

The rules you know

The right bower and the left bower, both counted as trump. Stick the dealer, or throw the deal in — your choice. Three or four tricks score one, all five score two, five alone score four, and a euchre gives the other side two. Play to 5, 7, 10 or 11. The rules page always says what your table plays.

Partners that play Euchre

Three levels. The computer players count the bowers, weigh whose hand the turned card goes to, read what a pass tells them and go alone when the hand is there. On Normal and Hard they play out possible deals before a call and before every card. They see only their own cards, as you do.

Easy to learn

Your first hands explain the turned card, the left bower and why a card can't be played. Claim the rest when every trick left is yours. Your record keeps games, wins, calls made, euchres, loners and hands of all five.

Honest money

An advert appears only between hands. An optional one-time purchase removes the adverts and adds two more decks: it changes how the cards look, not how the game plays.
