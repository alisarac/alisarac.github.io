How I made it

Fitting advertising into a game without breaking it

September 10, 2026 · Damga

Everything I had published before Damga was ad-free. I had never run AdMob in a product of my own and I wanted to see how it behaves, and a calm matching puzzle was a measured place to find out: in a game built on the player's attention, it shows immediately how far an advert can push in.

The order mattered

The usual way to add advertising is to finish the game and then look for places to put ads. Once you are in that order, every gap looks like a place — between levels, on the loss screen, on the way back to the menu, a strip above the board.

I went the other way. I wrote down what the game would never give up, and then fitted the advertising into what was left. The list was short:

  • The board is never covered.
  • The player never sees an advert at the moment they lose.
  • No video ever plays that the player did not ask for.

Those three lines ruled out most ad formats before I looked at any of them.

The rules that came out of it

No banner. No strip above or below the board. In Damga the position of the tiles is the game itself; permanently giving up part of the screen means shrinking the board, and shrinking the board means changing the puzzle.

A rewarded video only when you ask for one. It appears in two places: to earn another undo, or to get two tiles back off a full tray. Both are the player's own request. Never ask, and you never see a video in Damga.

Interstitials, in a narrow window. One can appear only when you finish a level. None at all for the first five — someone new meets the game through the game, not through an advert. After that, at most one every three levels, and never less than three minutes apart. Never after a loss.

That last rule is the arguable one, and it was not negotiable for me. Losing is already the game's punishment; adding thirty seconds of waiting on top of it puts people off more than the loss did.

What is actually being sold

A single purchase removes the between-level adverts for good. Rewarded videos keep working.

The distinction is deliberate. What you buy is the interruption, not the help the player asked for. Switching off the rewarded video too would take a capability away from the person who paid, and that capability is not an annoyance — it is part of the game.

What is left

No account, no sign-in, no analytics. Where you got to lives on your own phone and is erased from Settings in one tap. Google AdMob serves the adverts; apart from that, the game knows nothing about you.

What I learned was not technical. Wiring up AdMob takes an afternoon. The work was keeping to the rules after writing them: every limit produces a voice saying "one more would fit here", and the first time you listen to it the list stops meaning anything.
